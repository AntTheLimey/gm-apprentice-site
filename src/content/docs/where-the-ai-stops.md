description: You run the game, and you choose how much AI to use, part by part. What needs it, what doesn't, and what it never does by itself.

You run the game. You make the story, with your players. GM-Apprentice
keeps the books, and you choose how much of that you hand over. Some GMs
use all of it. You can use none of the AI and still publish a campaign site.

## Choose part by part

| | Without AI | With it |
|---|---|---|
| **Your notes** | Write them yourself. They're ordinary Markdown files in a folder you own. | It drafts, files and links notes when you ask. |
| **Checking** | Read them over yourself. | It looks for contradictions, dead NPCs who turn up again and clues with no backup. You decide what to do about each one. |
| **Pictures** | Use your own, or have none. | It draws what you ask for. Drawing without asking is off until you switch it on. |
| **Your players' website** | An ordinary program builds it from your notes. [GM-Scriptorium](/works-with/#gm-scriptorium) does the whole job without an agent. | The apprentice runs the build and the checks for you. |
| **Prep, the table, wrap-up** | You, as ever. | It preps, answers and writes up from your notes. This part is AI. |

GM-Scriptorium is a separate program by another GM. Its releases are early
and not signed yet, and the downloads are for Windows and Linux; on a Mac
you build it from source.

## What it doesn't do

- **It doesn't run your game.** It isn't an AI game master, and it doesn't
  talk to your players for you.
- **It doesn't own your notes.** Stop using it tomorrow and you still have a
  folder of Markdown.
- **It doesn't replace your books.** It carries open rules and short notes.
  For anything else, [you bring the books you own](/tutorials/your-own-books/).
- **It isn't for making things to sell.** Several publishers and stores don't
  take AI-made work. It's meant for your own table.

## What we'd want to know

- **Are the pictures on this site AI-drawn?** The painted ones are. The
  screenshots are real pages.
- **Are the stories on your campaign sites AI-written?** The apprentice
  drafts them from what happened at the table, and the GM decides what stays.
- **Did AI build this?** Much of it, yes, directed and tested by one GM who
  plays with it. The blog tells that story, mistakes included;
  [start here](/blog/stop-asking-the-model-to-be-the-machine/).
- **Where do my notes go?** They stay in your folder. The parts an agent
  reads go to whoever runs that agent, under your plan with them. A model on
  your own computer keeps them there.
- **Can it be wrong?** Yes. It can invent a detail or misread a rule. Check
  what matters, and keep the final say.
