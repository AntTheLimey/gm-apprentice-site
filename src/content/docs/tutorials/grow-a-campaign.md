The skill behind this is **the Midwife**. It's a conversation, not a form:
it asks one question at a time, offers a few answers to react to, and
writes down what you decide. At the end it writes an adventure brief and
sets up the campaign folder. It never decides for you; anything it invents
is marked as a draft until you accept it.

The quick version is [Start a new game](/start/new-game/). This page covers
how the conversation works, how to steer it, and the whole real run behind
that page.

## When to reach for it

- You want to run something and have no idea what.
- You have one image, a mood, or a premise and nothing else.
- Your players just finished an arc and you need the next one.
- You've got the premise but not the villain, the hook, or the ending.

Starting phrases that work:

- "I want to run a game but I have no idea what."
- "I keep thinking about a game set during a long sea voyage."
- "What's the natural next chapter, based on what's happened so far?"
- "This feels too big for a one-shot. Help me scope it down."

## How the conversation goes

It moves through four stages, though it adapts to what you bring:

1. **Discover** what excites you. Arrive with nothing and it offers seeds.
2. **Shape** the idea into something playable: the tension, the people, the
   ending.
3. **Structure** it: key NPCs, places, factions, what happens if the players
   do nothing.
4. **Set up** the folder for play: the brief, the Campaign Overview, notes
   for the people and places, your house rules.

Arrive with a fully formed concept and it skips ahead, stress-testing what
you have instead of starting from zero.

## Steering it

- **Each question comes with options. They're prompts, not a menu.** "None
  of these, but I like the bit about the tide" is a perfectly good answer.
- **Mix freely.** "The first one, but the keeper is still alive" is how the
  run below got its hook.
- **Tell it about your table.** Level, session count, and whether your
  players would rather talk or fight all change what it offers next.
- **Say when you've had enough.** "That's enough to run it, set it up" ends
  the questions. What's undecided goes on a list of open questions in the
  brief, for you, not it.
- **Nothing is lost.** Ideas you pass over go into a seed bank in
  `_midwife/seeds/`, and it brings relevant ones back next time you
  brainstorm.

### In a campaign you already run

Start the conversation inside the campaign's folder. The Midwife reads your
canon first, looks for unresolved threads, quiet factions and things you set
up but never used, and offers those as seeds for the next chapter.

### Keeping secrets

The brief has a player side and a GM side. The Campaign Overview holds only
what the players know at the start, and the twist lives under GM notes, so
publishing a campaign site later can't spoil it. The `_midwife/` folder is
never published.

## The whole run

From GM-Apprentice 1.12.98, in an empty folder. We typed the GM's side;
the replies are the Midwife's own, trimmed (`[...]`) but not reworded.

**Turn 1.**

> I want to run a short D&D 2024 one-shot for four players, something with a
> haunted lighthouse, but I've got nothing else yet

> [...] My first question is the one everything else hangs on: **what is
> the haunting actually about?** [...]
>
> 1. **The light is lying.** The old keeper died years ago, but the lamp
>    still burns every night, and now it steers ships *onto* the rocks. [...]
> 2. **It's haunted from the other direction.** The lighthouse doesn't keep
>    the dead *in*. It keeps the drowned *out*, and that seal is failing. [...]
> 3. **The haunting is a cover-up.** Smugglers or wreckers are faking a
>    ghost to keep the village away. But their act has woken something real
>    in the foundations [...]

**Turn 2.**

> Number 2, the drowned coming up the stairs a bit higher every night. I love
> that. My players are level 3 and like talking to NPCs more than fighting.
> One session, about four hours.

> [...] **One structural thought.** In a single session, "a bit higher every
> night" works best as the final night. The players arrive when the drowned
> are already halfway up the tower. Every bell, tide-turn or hour, they climb
> one more landing. That climb is your clock for the whole session, and the
> players can see it.
>
> For a table that likes talking, the most important question is next:
> **who are the drowned, and can they be reasoned with?** [...]
>
> 1. **The crew of one ship.** Forty years ago the keeper put the light out
>    on purpose so a ship would wreck. [...] The captain can be talked to,
>    but the crew are getting impatient. [...]

**Turn 3.**

> The first one, the crew of one ship. And the keeper is still alive, very
> old, living in the lighthouse. The players are hired by the keeper's
> granddaughter to find out why he's stopped answering letters.

> That's a strong hook. A worried granddaughter is a very human reason to go
> [...] It also gives you exactly three key NPCs, which is the right number
> for one session [...]
>
> Now the question the whole adventure turns on: **why did the keeper put
> out the light forty years ago?** [...]
>
> 2. **He was paid, and the village has been living on the money.** [...]
>    *The dilemma:* telling the truth satisfies the dead but ruins the
>    living, and that includes the person who hired the players. [...]

**Turn 4.**

> Option 2. Twist the knife. I think that's enough for me to run it, can you
> set it up so I can start prepping?

> Your campaign folder is set up and ready for prep, with the adventure
> written up in it. [...]
>
> - **A Campaign Overview** at `_Campaign/Campaign Overview.md` holds only
>   what the players know at the start, so none of the secret is in it. [...]
>
> **Last setup question:** do you have any house rules for this game [...]?

**Turn 5.**

> One house rule: a natural 1 on a death save counts as one failure, not two.
> And names: the keeper is Old Abel Marrow, the granddaughter is Wenna
> Marrow, the captain is Captain Isolde Crane of the Gannet, and the village
> is Saltreach.

> Your house rule and all the names are in, and the three NPCs and two
> places now have their own notes linked from the brief and overview. [...]
>
> - **Captain Isolde Crane of the Gannet:** her own stat block (CR 2), built
>   for a level 3 party. [...]
>   - She's designed to be beaten by talking, not by fighting. [...]

The brief it wrote opens like this:

> [[Old Abel Marrow]] has kept the light above [[Saltreach]] for longer than
> most of the village has been alive. Now he has stopped answering his
> granddaughter [[Wenna Marrow]]'s letters. [...] When they arrive, the light
> is still burning, but something is wrong with the tide, and wet footprints
> climb the tower stairs that weren't there the night before.

## What this grows into

[Snowbound](https://snowbound-dnd.pages.dev) started the same way: one
seed, "a mountain monastery shut in for the winter with a dozen pilgrims, a
murder on the first night, and the monks' secret". The Midwife turned it
into a brief, twelve NPCs, eight places and a four-session chapter. Four
sessions later it's the campaign site in the [gallery](/gallery/).
