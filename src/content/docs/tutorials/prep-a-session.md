The skill behind this is **session-prep**. It starts from what actually
happened last session (the wrap-up, not your old plan), reads the player
characters, the open threads and the NPCs, and builds a plan for the next
session. It asks you what the session is for, then does the legwork.

## When to reach for it

- A few days before the game.
- After the players went off-script and you need to know what still
  stands.
- When you're not sure whose turn it is for the spotlight.

Starting phrases that work:

- "Prep session 7."
- "Help me prep for Saturday."
- "Reconcile my session 6 prep with what actually happened."
- "Which threads are dangling that I should pick up?"

It works best after a [wrap-up](/tutorials/wrap-up-a-session/): the
wrap-up is what tells prep where things stand. If last session has none,
prep says so and offers to run one first.

## From a real run

On GM-Apprentice 1.12.98, using the [Snowbound sample vault](https://github.com/AntTheLimey/gm-apprentice-snowbound/tree/sample-v2)
(clone or download tag `sample-v2` to follow along), after the notes from
[Organize your notes](/tutorials/organize-your-notes/) were filed. We typed
the GM's side; the replies are the agent's own, trimmed (`[...]`) but not
reworded.

The GM has an idea for the next session and gives it in one line:

> [!you]
>
> Prep session 5. It's spring: the party walks down to Harrowford with
> Tam's body and the dragon coin.

Before writing anything, the apprentice checks that line against the
vault:

> [!apprentice]
>
> Before I write anything, your premise runs into four things the vault
> already says. Three of them I can settle with a default unless you'd
> rather go another way. The fourth is a real question.
>
> **Where your premise clashes with the vault**
>
> 1. **Chapter.** Chapter 1 is marked `complete` and the campaign is
>    `paused`. I'll file Session 5 under a new **Chapter 2 – Harrowford in
>    Spring** and set the campaign back to active.
> 2. **Tam's body.** The Session 4 recap only says Kael laid Tam out in
>    the chapel. But the Wrap-Up's thread summary and Kael's sheet both say
>    "Tam is buried." [...]
> 3. **Who walks down.** Kael and Grenna went down a month early and are
>    already with Hesta. [...]
> 4. **The open question: where does Hesta live?** [...]
>
> [...]
>
> **First question: what does Edda actually want from the coin?**
>
> - **(a) Only Tam's seventh.** She wants it to go to Hesta whole, with no
>   reeve's tithe and no creditors taking a cut, and she'll fight the
>   valley's own claims on it.
> - **(b) More than a seventh.** [...]
> - **(c) The coin as leverage.** [...]

The GM answers, and it asks one more thing (whether Edda's brother is
among the dead the party found). Then the GM hands it the rest:

> [!you]
>
> (a). Make the rest of the calls yourself and finish the plan. I'll read
> it through.

> [!apprentice]
>
> I've written the whole Session 5 Plan. It passes the plan check with no
> errors or warnings, [...]
>
> **Scenes, in likely order (none has to happen):**
>
> 1. **The House by the Mule-Yard.** Bram is at the gate and knows Tam's
>    coat before Hesta comes out. The mule that came back alone puts its
>    head over the rail. Hesta asks the party one question: "Did they make
>    him pay?"
> 2. **The Reeve's Tally.** Edda counts Tam's seventh onto Hesta's kitchen
>    table and waives her own tenth. [...]
> 3. **The Open Tab.** The old tab is still chalked on the board at the
>    Stag & Lantern. Rosie sits down beside Nim: "They know you're back." A
>    polite collector waits by the door. [...]
> 4. **The Burying.** Hesta asks Kael to speak over Tam. [...]
>
> [...]
>
> **Spotlight.** Ilyra and Nim haven't had a big moment yet, so they carry
> this one: the list and Edda's brother for Ilyra, the debt and Rosie for
> Nim. The other four get quieter beats: Kael speaks at the burial, Grenna
> stands beside a cured Hesta, Brannoc decides about Crane, and Oswin says
> the office if he comes down.
>
> **Calls I made, which you'll want to check as you read** (each is under
> Open Questions):
>
> - **Bram's claim.** Tam bought the mule on credit, which gives Edda a
>   claim from the valley itself to fight.
> [...]
>
> **You still need to settle:**
>
> - **Oswin.** Ask Dev whether Oswin walks down or stays with Wren. [...]
> - **Two names.** Edda's brother's name, as it reads on Ilyra's list, and
>   who Nim owes.

Every scene says what's going on, where, and how it starts, with who's
there and what they want. Most add the points to land and what happens
if the players do one thing or another. The opening of the first scene:

> [!vault]
>
> ### Scene 1: The House by the Mule-Yard
>
> **Situation:** The walkers come down the last switchback into
> [[Harrowford]] with [[Tam Rowe]] on a borrowed hurdle, and the mule-yard
> is the first place in town they pass.
>
> [...]
>
> **Points to land**
>
> - [ ] The mule came back down the pass alone in the autumn, and Bram has
>   fed it all winter.
> - [ ] Hesta is well: the blue has gone from her lips.
> - [ ] Hesta's question, "Did they make him pay?", gets put to the party
>   (her letter: "If the brothers want paying, don't pay it").
>
> [...]
>
> Read-aloud: The last switchback comes out above wet thatch and
> woodsmoke. The mud in the road is the first soft thing underfoot since
> autumn. At the mule-yard a man leans on the gate with a halter over his
> arm, and behind him a brown mule lifts its head from the hay and watches
> the hurdle come down the hill.

## What's in a plan

- **What the session is for**, in your words, and a "previously on".
- **Threads in play** and the **world as it stands**: who's where, who's
  alive, what's ticking.
- **Scenes**, each with its people and how it starts, and usually the
  points to land and an "If the players..." table. None of them has to
  happen.
- **Fallback scenes** for the likely detours.
- **Every player character**, where their story stands and the hook on
  the table for each one, offered, never forced. No PC is left without
  one.
- **Open questions**: the calls only you (or a player) can make, and any
  it made because you told it to. What a player chooses for their
  character is prepped as an "If the players..." row, not decided for
  them.

It also tidies what your premise moves. Here it opened Chapter 2, fixed
the "Tam is buried" lines, and added stat blocks for the NPCs the party
is likely to cross.

## Other things to ask it

- "What did we skip from my prep? Salvage what's still good."
- "Which PC hasn't had the spotlight recently?"
- "Which clues did the players miss that they still need to find?"
- "What ticking clocks are about to go off?"
