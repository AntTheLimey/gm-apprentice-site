The skill behind this is **ttrpg-expert**: rules advisor, content maker
and continuity checker in one. It knows five systems: D&D 5e (2024),
Pathfinder 2e (Remaster), Call of Cthulhu 7e, GURPS 4e and Forged in the
Dark. It can help with others in general terms. When you have a vault, it
reads it first, so what it makes fits what's already there.

## When to reach for it

- You need a person, a place, a faction, an item or a creature.
- You want a handout: a letter, a diary page, a notice on a door.
- You're building a character, or checking one against its point budget.
- A rules question needs a proper answer, not a quick one.
- You want a scenario outline, an encounter, or a few ideas to get you
  unstuck.

Starting phrases that work:

- "Make me an NPC for the inn's kitchen."
- "Write the letter the cult leader left on the desk."
- "Build me a 150-point GURPS detective."
- "How does grappling work in the 2024 rules?"
- "Give me three ideas for what's in the cellar."

## From a real run

On GM-Apprentice 1.12.98, using the [Snowbound sample vault](https://github.com/AntTheLimey/gm-apprentice-snowbound/tree/sample-v2)
(clone or download tag `sample-v2` to follow along), after the session in
[Wrap up a session](/tutorials/wrap-up-a-session/). We typed the GM's
side; the replies are the agent's own, trimmed (`[...]`) but not reworded.

> [!you]
>
> Make me an NPC for the Stag & Lantern's kitchen. Someone the party will
> want to come back to.

> [!apprentice]
>
> # Joss Applegarth, cook at the Stag & Lantern
>
> **Concept:** The man who feeds you and always charges you for it,
> exactly. After a winter in a house that fed the party for free and took
> their lives as payment, he's the honest version of that kindness.
>
> **Draft. Nothing is filed in the vault yet.**
>
> ### At the table
>
> - **Look:** Late forties, broad, flour up to the elbows. His forearms
>   are shiny with old burns. There's always chalk dust on his knuckles.
> - **Speech:** He talks in prices and portions. "Two bowls, one heel of
>   bread, a penny for the dripping." Short, dry, never unkind.
> - **Tell:** He sets a full bowl down in front of you before you've
>   ordered and chalks it up before you've taken a spoonful.
> - **Line:** "Up there they didn't charge you. Think on that. Down here
>   it's on the slate."
>
> [...]
>
> ### Why the party comes back
>
> 1. **The road-parcel (homebrew).** It's bread, hard cheese and a twist
>    of salt pork tied in one of his blue cloths, 5 sp on the slate. On a
>    Short Rest, a PC who eats one rolls their first spent Hit Point Die at
>    its maximum. He wants the cloth back.
>
> [...]
>
> - **Secrets:**
>   - *Surface:* The back of the slate is covered in old marks that were
>     never wiped. Some of the chalk has gone grey.
>   - *Investigation:* He was the kitchen boy twenty years ago. The old
>     cook taught him the slate, and he packed **Wat Marsh's** parcel
>     himself. Edda has never asked him about it, and he's never told her.
>   - *Deep:* The slate is the valley's own register. Checked against
>     Ilyra's list, a name on the slate but not in the register means
>     someone died on the pass, not at the house. [...]
>
> ### Optional hooks (your call)
>
> [...]
>
> - **Mattias Pell:** Nim ran out through *his* back door, and Mattias had
>   been waiting in Joss's kitchen for an hour. "Paid for his tea. Exact.
>   Asked for a receipt." The name on that receipt could settle who Mattias
>   works for, if you want it to.
>
> ### Stats: Commoner (CR 0), reflavoured
>
> [...]
>
> I checked "Joss Applegarth" against the vault and no existing name comes
> close to it. [...]
>
> Want me to file him into the vault? [...] Tell me which of the optional
> hooks to keep before it goes in.

Wat Marsh is the reeve's brother, who went up the pass twenty years ago
and never came back. The GM named him in the last session's notes, and the
new cook ties straight into him.

> [!you]
>
> File him. Keep the receipt hook, drop the Gilt one.

> [!apprentice]
>
> I've filed Joss Applegarth with the receipt hook and dropped the Gilt
> hook. [...]
>
> - **`Characters/NPCs/Joss Applegarth.md`**: the players' part has the
>   overview and his appearance. The GM Notes have the stat block, the
>   road-parcel rule, how to play him, what he wants and knows, the three
>   moves, his secrets, and the receipt hook.
> - **`Items & Artifacts/The Kitchen Slate.md`**: the players' part only
>   says what the slate is for. The old grey marks, Wat's and Tam's marks,
>   and how to check it against the list are in the GM Notes.
>
> [...]
>
> **Pictures:** Drawing is switched on for this vault, so Codex drew a
> portrait of Joss and a picture of the slate, at no cost. [...]

![Joss Applegarth, the cook: a broad, bearded man in a floury apron, arms folded, with a cooking fire behind him](/img/tutorials/joss-applegarth.jpg)

Then a rules question:

> [!you]
>
> Rules check: can Nim use Cunning Action to Hide while the collector is
> looking straight at her?

> [!apprentice]
>
> **No, not while he has a clear view of her.** Cunning Action only makes
> Hide a Bonus Action instead of an Action. The requirements for hiding
> stay the same.
>
> [...]
>
> If the collector is staring at her across open ground, she can't attempt
> it.
>
> **How she can hide anyway:**
>
> - **Break line of sight first.** She ducks behind the bar, a door or a
>   stack of barrels, then uses Cunning Action to Hide. Thanks to Cunning
>   Action she can move and hide in the same turn.
> - **Naturally Stealthy (halfling, on her sheet).** She can Hide when the
>   only thing obscuring her is a creature at least one size larger than
>   her. Stepping behind a Medium person counts, so Joss would work and
>   Rosie wouldn't, since Rosie is also Small. [...]
>
> [...]

It answered from Nim's own sheet, and used the cook it had just made.

## What it does

- **NPCs at three depths:** a three-line sketch for a walk-on, a fuller
  write-up (what they want, how they act, their moves and secrets) for
  someone who'll recur, and a full treatment for a central figure. Stat
  blocks come with them.
- **Places, factions, items, creatures, encounters and scenarios**, built
  from ready-made templates.
- **Handouts** in a voice: letters, diaries, newspaper pieces, telegrams.
- **Characters to the rules**, with point budgets tracked and checked.
- **Rules answers** that cite the bundled reference by file and line,
  and say when they go past it.
- **Continuity checks:** plot holes, forgotten threads, clues that lead
  nowhere.
- **Nothing is filed until you say so.** What it makes is a draft in the
  conversation. It checks new names against the vault. (In our run it also marked the
  road-parcel, which it made up, as homebrew.)

The rules it carries are from free, open sources (the D&D 5.2 SRD, the
Pathfinder ORC books, the Blades in the Dark SRD, and Basic Roleplaying
under the ORC License, plus the plugin's own descriptions of Call of
Cthulhu's mechanics) and, for GURPS, point costs and short notes
under Steve Jackson Games' online policy. For books you own, see
[Your own rulebooks](/tutorials/your-own-books/).
