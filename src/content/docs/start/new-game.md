You have an idea, or half of one, or nothing at all. In about five replies
the Midwife turns it into a campaign folder: an adventure brief, the people
and places with their own notes, your house rules, and a list of what's
still yours to decide. Then you're ready to prep the first session.

Everything below is from a real run on GM-Apprentice 1.12.98. We typed the
GM's side for this page; the replies are the Midwife's own, trimmed (`[...]`)
but not reworded.

New to all this? [Get set up](/start/setup/) first.

## 1. Make an empty folder and open your agent in it

One folder per campaign. Call it anything; you can rename it later. If you
use Obsidian, open the same folder as a vault.

## 2. Say what you've got

However little it is. You don't need to name the Midwife: say you want to
run a game and it comes.

> [!you]
>
> I want to run a short D&D 2024 one-shot for four players, something with a
> haunted lighthouse, but I've got nothing else yet

> [!apprentice]
>
> A haunted lighthouse is a good place to start. It's one building with one
> way up and the sea on every side, which is exactly the kind of closed
> setting a one-shot needs. [...]
>
> My first question is the one everything else hangs on: **what is the
> haunting actually about?** Here are three possible answers to react to.
> [...]
>
> 2. **It's haunted from the other direction.** The lighthouse doesn't keep
>    the dead *in*. It keeps the drowned *out*, and that seal is failing.
>    Every night the tide brings the drowned a little higher up the stairs.

## 3. Pick, mix or refuse

Each question comes with a few answers to react to. Take one, change one,
combine two, or say none of them fit. Tell it about your table while you're
at it.

> [!you]
>
> Number 2, the drowned coming up the stairs a bit higher every night. I love
> that. My players are level 3 and like talking to NPCs more than fighting.
> One session, about four hours.

It shapes the next question around what you said: a table that likes
talking gets ghosts that can be talked to. Two more rounds settled who the
drowned are (the crew of one wrecked ship), who hires the players (the
keeper's granddaughter) and why the keeper put the light out forty years
ago (the wreckers paid him, and the village was built on the money).

## 4. Say when you've got enough

> [!you]
>
> Option 2. Twist the knife. I think that's enough for me to run it, can you
> set it up so I can start prepping?

> [!apprentice]
>
> Your campaign folder is set up and ready for prep, with the adventure
> written up in it. [...]
>
> **Parts I wrote that you haven't agreed to yet**
> - **The night-long clock:** it all happens on one night, and the drowned
>   climb one landing at each bell or tide-turn. [...]
>
> **Still for you to decide** (listed as Open Questions in the brief):
> - Names for the keeper, granddaughter, captain, ship and village. [...]
>
> **Last setup question:** do you have any house rules for this game [...]?

## 5. Answer the last questions

> [!you]
>
> One house rule: a natural 1 on a death save counts as one failure, not two.
> And names: the keeper is Old Abel Marrow, the granddaughter is Wenna
> Marrow, the captain is Captain Isolde Crane of the Gannet, and the village
> is Saltreach.

The three NPCs and two places got notes of their own, each NPC with a stat
block built for the party. The captain's is built to be beaten by talking:

> [!apprentice]
>
> - **Rewards honesty:** her fear attack automatically fails against anyone
>   who has told the truth.

## What you have now

```text
Adventures/Haunted Lighthouse/Haunted Lighthouse.md   the brief: player side and GM side
_Campaign/Campaign Overview.md                        what the players know, and your house rules
Characters/NPCs/Old Abel Marrow.md
Characters/NPCs/Wenna Marrow.md
Characters/NPCs/Captain Isolde Crane.md
Locations/...                                         Saltreach and its lighthouse
Chapters/Chapter 1/Chapter 1.md
_midwife/                                             the conversation's notes, and the ideas you didn't pick
```

Everything is plain Markdown with links between the notes. Anything the
Midwife invented is marked as a draft until you accept it.
